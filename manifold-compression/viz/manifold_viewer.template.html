How to read this

Inside the model, each token is a list of 1,024 numbers, a point in a 1,024-dimensional space. You can't draw that, so the view picks the 3 directions along which the tokens differ most at this point (principal components) and plots each token's position along them.

This is a shadow. Those 3 directions hold only about an eighth of the real spread; reaching 90% takes around 500 of the 1,024 directions. Clumps and arms you see are real, but points that look close may be far apart in directions you can't see.

Why it's a cloud, not a surface. A smooth sheet is 2-dimensional. This manifold is about 10 to 16 dimensional (see "Intrinsic dimension"), curved through 1,024 dimensions. The shadow of something that thick fills in like a solid, the way the shadow of a crumpled paper ball is filled even though the paper is thin.

The fitted sheet is a smooth surface that predicts direction 3 from directions 1 and 2, estimated from the dots. It is the curve-approximation idea in miniature: guess a dropped direction from the kept ones. The readout says how much of direction 3 it explains. High means the cloud really is sheet-like here; low means the sheet is mostly fiction.

The cloud is rotated at each step to line up with the step before, so as you move through the layers the motion you see is the representation changing, not the camera spinning.

Paths through depth follows 40 tokens through all 49 points on one shared set of axes. Each line is one token's journey from its embedding to the output; the bright dots mark where they are at the selected point.

Why this matters

This view comes from an experiment on shrinking models. If the tokens only used a few directions, you could cut the model's width almost for free. They don't: the spread is wide. But it isn't random either. The measurements below show that part of what a width cut throws away can be predicted from what it keeps, because the cloud is curved rather than flat. That predictable part is the "curve" a smaller model would need to learn.

Qwen3.5-0.8B, 33,726 tokens of encyclopedia text, Python, grade-school maths, tool-calling chat, and a code-recall task. 1,500 tokens shown, the same ones at every point.
