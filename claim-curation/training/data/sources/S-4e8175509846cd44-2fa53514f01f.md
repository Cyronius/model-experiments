# bfp16 GEMM: the dataflow is the bound, the K-chunk body is load-bound, and on-core q4 dequant costs as much as the matmul

All on `open_kernels/designs/gemm_bfp16` (whole-array bfp16 x bfp16 -> fp32, 32 cores, core tile 64 x 64 x 128, one K chunk = 1024 8x8x8 mmuls a core). Probes in `utilities/gemm-peak/` and `utilities/aie-probes/mmul_rate_probe.*` of that branch.

## Dataflow against arithmetic (performance mode, busy box, min of 20)

`GB_NULL=1` compiles the kernel bodies to an immediate return, every DMA kept. 256 tokens a dispatch:

| shape (N x K) | null (no arithmetic) ms | full ms |
|---|---|---|
| 4096 x 2560 | 0.886 | 0.671-1.023 |
| 4096 x 4096 | 1.002 | 1.121 |
| 14336 x 4096 | 2.819 | 3.498 |
| 4096 x 14336 | 2.704 | 2.626 |

The null build is as slow as the full one: the GEMM is bound by data movement, not by the cores. Full builds read 6-11 TFLOPS in this state (performance mode; another session's NPU work between units).

## DDR rate, one stream a column against two (performance mode, same unit)

`dma_probe.py`: each column streams 160 objects from DDR through its memtile to its 4 cores (broadcast), cores only acquire and release. 8 streams (one a column): 20.8-23.9 GB/s (min of 20). 16 streams (both MM2S channels of every column, the cores' second input channel): 17.3-22.8 GB/s. Two channels a column do not add rate: the ceiling is shared, not per channel (agrees with the 2026-09-22 probe in this repo's notes, at a lower absolute in this state).

## The K-chunk body, static (Peano, -O2, optimization records)

`mm_f32_mid` as shipped (nested z / j loops, 2x2 expansion, inner k loop unrolled by 2): about 87 bundles per (row pair, column pair) block of 32 mmuls, i.e. ~2800 cycles a K chunk (37 % of one mmul a cycle). The postpipeliner reports for a flattened form (one loop over the 32 blocks, k octets fully unrolled): MII 52, achieved II 69, NS 1; the loads (16 accumulator loads + 32 block-vector pops + fills a block) set the MII. A 2x4 expansion spills (147 spill lines): 8 live 64-lane fp32 accumulators do not fit.

## Measured rates of one core (turbo unless said, min of 36 dispatches, slope between two iteration counts)

- int8 8x8x8 mmul, operands in L1: 0.625 ns (0.91 ns in performance mode).
- bfp16 8x8x8 mmul, unpipelined probe: 4.8 ns.
- The bfp16 K-chunk body (`mm_f32_mid` on L1 tiles): 1.54 us (performance mode) to 2.2 us (turbo, noisier unit) a chunk.

## Dequantising q4 pool chunks to a bfp16 B tile on the core (`designs/gemm_bfp16/q4_bfp.h`)

Converting one 64-k quarter of four q4_1 chunks (128 rows, 8192 weights) to the 128 x 64 bfp16 B tile: first version 1.99 us measured (2.07 us with f32 scales); the rewritten loop (16-x-8 byte transpose, `interleave_zip` with 0x43 for bf16 128 + q, `m - 128 d` accumulator, one output stream) is 85 bundles a 512-weight step, MII 50 (shuffle-bound), ~1360 cycles a quarter static, 1.37 us measured (1.34 us with f32 scales). That is about one K chunk's matmul again; the shuffle unit (unpack, transpose, zip, rep8 patterns) is the bound. The store `vst.push.576.conv.bfp16ebs8.fp32` converts an fp32 accumulator to bfp16 on the way out at no extra op.

## The K-chunk body, flattened

One loop over the 32 (row pair, column pair) blocks with the k octets unrolled: 69 bundles a block against the nested form's ~87, bit-identical output (rel_fro 0 against the decoded-operand product at six 8B shapes). On hardware it is no faster, because the dataflow is the bound.

## 8B shapes, best of 20-30 (256 tokens a dispatch, busy box, turbo and performance units mixed)

N x K: 4096 x 4096 0.88-0.95 ms (9.0-9.7 TFLOPS); 6144 x 4096 1.26-1.34 ms (9.6-10.3); 12288 x 4096 2.28-2.33 ms (11.1-11.3); 14336 x 4096 2.68-2.80 ms (10.7-11.2); 4096 x 12288 2.42 ms (10.6); 4096 x 14336 2.67-2.77 ms (10.8-11.3).

## Whole model (Qwen3-8B, performance mode)

The bfp16 route prefills 426 tok/s at 1024 tokens and 457 tok/s at 2048 (bf16 route: ~103). The dit_fa prep stream (`dfp*`, 32 heads, q|k|v padded to 6144) hangs at layer 0 with ERT state 8 after ~7 s on this set; with the prep on the host (`OFLM_OPEN_DIT_PREP=0`) the route runs. Not root-caused.

## Reading

The q4 weights would cut DDR bytes a K step by ~37 % (B 5120 B against 9216 B a column), but the dequant adds ~50-60 % to the core's work, and the core and the DMA are already within ~30 % of each other: on-core q4 dequant does not speed this GEMM up with Peano's code; it saves the bfp16 copy's memory.
